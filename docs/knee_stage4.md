## 2. Revised senescence logic and calibration (GSE104782 OA1–5, SenMayo target)

* `sen_overload_weight` w — overload-driven senescence is multiplied by w + (1 − w) · inflammation: with w < 1 overload alone induces less senescence and inflammatory co-signals are needed (cf. stage 2 for the switch);
* `senescent_death_rate` — senescent cells die (apoptosis) at this daily hazard.

**Neither senescence revision was selected; the stage-4 model is the stage-2 model.**

Why (calibration patients, loss by readout):

| model | loss SENMAYO | loss RUNX2 | loss MMP13 | loss SOX9 | senescent, stage 0 | senescent, stage 4 | RUNX2-high, stage 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| stage 2 | 104 | 35.8 | 27 | 11.6 | 0.143 | 1 | 0.0907 |
| gated, w = 0.3 | 142 | 31.9 | 45.9 | 15.1 | 0.087 | 1 | 0 |

Gating lowers senescence in intact tissue but not in degraded tissue, where cytokines are high anyway, so the damaged/intact ratio gets *worse*; and with less senescence there is less cytokine, so fewer cells switch to RUNX2-high and the catabolic (MMP13) fit deteriorates. In this architecture the senescent cells' SASP is the only inflammatory source inside cartilage, which couples senescence to catabolism; the data show catabolism without massive senescence, pointing to an inflammatory source outside cartilage (synovium).

MMP13 is strongly higher only when OA knees are compared with *normal* knees; within OA joints it is not higher (slightly lower in affected than in preserved cartilage) and against meniscus-tear knees it does not differ. Catabolism behaves as a property of the whole OA joint, not of the local lesion — consistent with a joint-level (synovial) inflammatory source and inconsistent with the model, which localises catabolism to damaged tissue via the SASP of senescent cells. No senescence readout shows the large increase the model predicts.

## 7. Conclusions

* **The senescence failure is real.** A 124-gene SASP/senescence score that shares no gene with CDKN2A gives the same picture as p16 in three datasets: late, moderate senescence in damaged tissue.
* **The two revisions of the senescence logic were not selected** by the calibration; the model is unchanged from stage 2.
* **Mechanism:** senescence and catabolism are coupled through the SASP, the model's only intra-cartilage inflammatory source; reducing senescence removes the catabolic co-signal. Across four datasets catabolism is a joint-level feature (OA vs normal knees), not a lesion-level one (within OA joints). The next hypothesis to test is a joint-level (synovial) inflammatory input that drives catabolism throughout the joint without senescence.
* **Pre-registered test (GSE117999):** stage 1 0 / 5, stage 2 (= 3) 2 / 5 readouts consistent (C1).

## 1. Hypothesis and revisions

Stage 4 found that catabolism (MMP13) is a joint-level OA feature (OA vs normal knees) rather than a lesion-level one, and that in the model the senescent cells' SASP was the only inflammatory source in cartilage. Stage 5 adds:

* `C_joint` — a joint-level cytokine background (synovial inflammation) throughout the cartilage of OA joints (0 in normal joints);
* `k_inf` — inflammation-driven catabolism, catabolic drive += k_inf · C/(K_C + C) (IL-1 / TNF → NF-κB → MMP13);
* `sen_infl_gain` — how strongly cytokines amplify the senescence hazard (2 before; 0 = not at all).

Calibration loss = within-joint stage ratios (GSE104782 OA1–5, SenMayo target) + between-joint OA/normal ratios (GSE114007 raw counts, RNA-seq). Both datasets were seen before, so everything in sections 2–3 is calibration or consistency, not an independent test.

## 6. Post hoc caveat (does not change the registered result)

Woetzel et al. (2014) list *joint trauma patients* among the control group, so part of the 'healthy' synovium comes from acutely injured joints. That is consistent with IL1B and IL6 being *lower* in OA than in controls, and it makes these data a weak test of the premise in either direction: the registered result is 'not supported', not 'refuted'. A cleaner test needs non-traumatic, non-arthritic control synovium.

## 7. Conclusions

* **Not selected.** Joint-level inflammation and inflammation-driven catabolism improve the between-joint fit (best between-joint loss 79 vs 93 for stage 2) but worsen the within-joint cell fit, and the total calibration loss prefers the stage-2 model.
* **Premise not supported** in the pre-registered synovium test (OA − healthy inflammation score -0.09 [-0.24, +0.07]), with a valid RA positive control; the control group included trauma patients (section 6).
* **The largest remaining gap is between joints:** OA vs normal cartilage shows SOX9 -2.2 and MMP13 +3.6 log2, the model -0.1 and +1.4, with far more senescence. Whatever separates OA from normal joints (in these data also age and tissue source) is not captured by local overload plus SASP, and these data do not show that it is synovial inflammation.
* **Where this leaves the model:** across five stages, only the stage-2 revisions survived calibration; every later hypothesis (stages 3-5) failed to improve the calibration on the open data. Progress now needs data that are not open without an agreement (OAI longitudinal imaging and biomarkers) or new paired cartilage–synovium cohorts with non-traumatic controls.
